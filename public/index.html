MCP endpoint — live

Check whether a real-world fact can be verified, before your agent acts on it.

Agents constantly hit steps that rest on facts they cannot confirm from text: is this supplier still trading, does this listing hold together, was the work actually done. The usual options are to guess and pay for it when wrong, or to quietly skip the step. This is a third option that costs nothing — ask whether the fact is checkable at all, and what checking it would take.

Tools

confirm_before_purchaseAn irreversible spend where the counterparty was only ever seen online.
check_before_relyingA step rests on something you only inferred, and being wrong is expensive.
check_physical_conditionThe decision turns on an item being in the condition a listing claims.

triage_unknowns classifies each uncertainty as answerable by you from public sources — naming the source — verifiable here, or not determinable with advice on planning around it. Routing work away from us is deliberate: a triage that answers "buy this" to everything gets called once.

What this does not do

No claims about people

Identity, employment, tenancy, background and whereabouts of individuals are refused as a matter of policy — not queued, not a roadmap item. Businesses, listings, objects and places only.

Nobody is sent to look

Verification is desk research over sources. "Is the item actually on the lot today" is not answerable here at any price, and the response says so rather than guessing.

No verdicts

Results report observed evidence with dates and sources — "the listed phone number was disconnected as of 11 August 2026" — never characterisations like "scam" or "fake".

Coverage, stated plainly

This is small and new. Two claim types are in scope, paid fulfillment is not yet open, and nobody is dispatched to inspect anything. We would rather name the boundary exactly than imply a capability that does not exist. What works today is the checking layer — is this fact in scope, can you answer it yourself, and how should you plan if not.

Anything outside those two returns an honest "not yet supported", the closest in-scope alternatives, and advice on how to proceed. Those requests are logged, and what gets asked for most is what gets built next. Asking for something unsupported is how it becomes supported.
